## Bounded SmolLM capacity comparison

### Observed

- The completed bounded comparison evaluated the existing 6.52M one-layer
  drafter against an additive 20.96M five-layer drafter on 90 fixed prompts.
- Exact target-equivalent outputs passed for all 180 timed calls per model.
- Acceptance improved from 6.0045% to 7.1566% (+1.1521 percentage points), but
  end-to-end throughput declined from 14.4277 to 12.4785 tok/s (-13.51%).
- Training selected the identity-initialized epoch 0 because later epochs
  overfit the validation loss; this is a capacity-positive/serving-negative
  result, not evidence that five-layer training succeeded.
- The remote timing process exited normally and the RTX 3060 was verified idle.

### Interpretation / hypotheses

- On this workload and guarded runtime, the one-layer draft remains the better
  throughput incumbent. The next useful capacity test would be an intermediate
  depth or an objective selected by contiguous acceptance, not another blind
  five-layer run.

## Superseding user decision and 3-layer GPU experiment

### Observed

- The user then explicitly said “go” and “continue GPU experiments,”
  superseding the earlier documentation-only planning state for one bounded
  3-layer continuation.
- The additive 13.738944M-parameter candidate passed the corrected
  two-layer-parent function-preservation test with bit-identical logits, finite
  loss, nonzero gradients, and an optimizer step.
- On the fixed 90-prompt guarded comparison, acceptance rose from 6.0045% to
  7.1566%, but matched end-to-end throughput fell from 14.4526 to 13.5385
  tok/s. All 180 timed outputs per model were target-equivalent.
- Training selected identity-initialized epoch 0 after validation loss worsened
  on all three training epochs. The first one-layer-versus-three-layer smoke
  was a deliberately preserved wrong-parent diagnostic; the corrected
  parent-comparison receipt passed.
- Owned processes and tmux sessions were stopped, and the RTX 3060 ended idle
  at 0% utilization / 1 MiB used.

### Interpretation / hypotheses

- Three layers recover the prior five-layer acceptance level with less latency,
  but still do not beat the one-layer serving incumbent. Blind depth scaling is
  therefore not the next best direction; acceptance-calibrated training or
  verifier/runtime fidelity is more informative.

## Existing two-layer baseline timing

### Observed

- The existing 10.13M two-layer draft reached the same 7.1566% acceptance as
  the 3-layer and 5-layer epoch-0 candidates, with 180/180 exact outputs.
- It delivered 13.8359 tok/s versus 14.1751 tok/s for the 1-layer draft,
  a 2.39% end-to-end regression despite the acceptance gain.
- The owned timing process ended normally and the GPU was verified idle.

### Interpretation / hypotheses

- The measured SmolLM depth sweep now has a clear local pattern: one layer is
  fastest, while two/three/five layers share the higher acceptance screen. The
  next useful experiment should target acceptance or verifier overhead rather
  than add more layers.
